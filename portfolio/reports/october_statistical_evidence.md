# October 2024 subway entry patterns — statistical evidence

**Author:** Cyril James Lunzaga
**Data owner:** Metropolitan Transportation Authority
**Source:** [MTA Subway Hourly Ridership: 2020–2024](https://data.ny.gov/d/wujg-7c2s)

## Day-level variation

Observed mean daily weekday entries were 4,128,696, compared with 2,465,017 on weekends.
The weekday/weekend ratio was 1.67, with a 95% within-month day-resampling interval [1.54, 1.83].

## Rush-hour timing

Across 23 observed weekdays, evening minus morning share averaged 3.08 percentage points per weekday (95% day-resampling interval [2.44, 3.84]).
Evening entries exceeded morning entries on 23 of those days.
Manhattan's day-level evening-minus-morning imbalance exceeded that of the combined Bronx, Brooklyn, and Queens by 27.61 percentage points on average (95% day-resampling interval [26.48, 28.61]).

## Station peak orientation

424 of 424 station complexes met the predeclared 90% peak-hour coverage threshold.
Among 424 stations eligible under primary and alternative equal-length windows, 88.9% kept the same orientation label.
Orientation counts: balanced=72, evening-oriented=91, morning-oriented=261.

## Interpretation and limits

These are estimated fare-paid station entries, not individual commuter trips, crowding, train loads, or station-to-station journeys. Date resampling describes variability within one historical month; its intervals are not forecasts or causal evidence. Time dependence, holidays, and changes in service are not controlled. Typology thresholds and alternative time windows are explicitly conventional. Missing station-hours are not imputed as zero, and representative coordinates remain nonauthoritative.
